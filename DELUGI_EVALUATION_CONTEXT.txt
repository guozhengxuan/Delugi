WHAT DELUGI IS TRYING TO ACHIEVE

Central motivation:
  Reducing dissemination cost while keeping ordering efficient and robust.

The two contributions to implement and evaluate are:
1. Cumulative prefix availability with ordinary signatures: a receiver's signed
   observation covers many accumulated batches, and MMR proofs combine unequal
   observations into evidence for exact selected prefixes.
2. A pipelined ordering protocol that supports a ready three-delay commit path
   and one-delay proposal spacing, avoids metadata requests for decisions and
   resolution, and preserves sufficiently late honest proposals despite faults.

The experiments should establish where this combination helps, its actual
costs and bottlenecks, and where counterparts perform better. Useful transaction
progress matters more than counting committed empty or non-advancing proposals.

Implement these modules, with clear interfaces:

- Producer streams, authenticated batches, persistent or explicitly configured
  in-memory storage, chunked MMR state, and consistency-proof construction.
- Observation vectors, current/historical witness handling, frontier construction,

- A deterministic execution/materialization workload that can verify every

- Instrumentation, fault injection, local/deployment runners, baseline adapters,

PROTOCOL DETAILS THAT MUST SURVIVE IMPLEMENTATION

Read the actual algorithms; this is only a checklist against accidental redesign.
The model has n=3f+1, ordinary signatures/hashes, and partial synchrony. Lowercase
delta denotes actual delay; uppercase Delta is the configured known bound.

Dissemination:
- Producers stream consecutive authenticated batches without a receipt-quorum
  gate on the next batch. Maintain exact branch-bound MMR checkpoints.
- A replica signs one cumulative n-stream observation vector per reporting
  opportunity and immutable named base. The leader collects n-f vectors.
- Each selected suffix uses f+1 compatible witnesses. This guarantees at least
  one honest holder; it does not mean all replicas possess the payload.
- Preserve the honest source-holding constructor: the leader must hold the
  source suffix, reconstruct its checkpoint, and have the nodes needed to
  construct proofs. Do not replace this with a root-only selector.
- Keep the specified n-coordinate frontier/current-evidence rule, historical
  source authentication, base-equality cases, retrieval, and retention behavior.
  Finite pruning is an extension; execution alone does not end custody.

Ordering:
- Use immutable, local, total Q(r,x,pi), without fetching during verification
  or consulting a mutable local delivered frontier.
- Implement PROPOSE, VOTE, CER, HISTORY3, PROG, CLOSED, terminal CER/NO-CER
  outcomes, all timer guards, old-round handlers, and scanner behavior.
- n-f VOTEs justify CER, f+1 CERs amplify CER, and n-f CERs directly commit.
  A first VOTE requires full validation; CER amplification does not waive it.
- VOTEs carry the signed header and descriptor x, not the whole witness/history.
  CERs remain short. Preserve the once-only signing/sending rules.
- HISTORY3 uses exact closed endpoints and the three greatest own CER positions.
  The scanner waits at unknown authorities and never requests decision metadata.
- Fixed round-robin leadership and recurring honest triples matter to ordered
  release. Do not silently replace them with a different leader policy.

Some integration semantics remain research obligations: shared named bases,
timely proof/input construction, concurrent Byzantine branch compatibility, and
full execution guarantees. Identify a concrete missing rule when it affects
code, propose the smallest faithful resolution, and ask the author for the
specific design decision if necessary. Continue implementing independent parts.
Do not use these open items as a reason to stop at a plan. Start with an explicit
honest-network integration slice and expand its supported fault model honestly;
do not advertise a restricted prototype as unrestricted BFT.

EXPERIMENTS FOR THE PAPER'S EVALUATION SECTION

1. Fault-free performance and scalability.
   Produce throughput-versus-latency curves over increasing offered load, from
   low load through saturation. Compare committee sizes, network conditions,
   transaction sizes, and batching. Report sustainable useful throughput,
   p50/p95/p99 latency, CPU, bandwidth, and memory. Start with 4/7-node local
   runs; expand to larger committees and WAN deployment when resources permit.

2. Dissemination cost and amortization.
   Measure actual signatures, verification/proof-generation time, evidence bytes,
   payload bytes, and total traffic per useful ordered batch/transaction. Vary
   accumulation per reporting opportunity, reporting rate, MMR height, uneven
   observations, and historical evidence. Include branch-search cost under
   producer forks. Find break-even points against fairly batched certification.

3. Correct leaders with message loss or delayed payloads.
   Run random/burst loss, partial delivery, and slow-replica experiments. Distinguish
   payload-only loss from control/mixed loss and apply comparable conditions to
   every protocol. The Raptr/Shoal++ motivation includes 1% egress drops at five
   of 100 nodes and roughly 10x Mysticeti latency in that experiment. Reproduce
   the setting where feasible; do not assume the same result in advance.
   Specify packet/connection/application loss and retransmission behavior.

4. Correct leaders with Byzantine participants.
   Implement selective publication, withholding, and timed interference. Measure
   useful throughput, full-proposal preservation, repair, and timeout dependence.
   Vary actual network delay with Delta fixed. Reproduce Raptr's easy-spoiling
   attack from Multimmit Section 3.3: one early withheld batch suppresses later
   available batches across producers; k faulty producers can spoil k consecutive
   proposals and force affected honest traffic onto the certified slow path.
   Measure useful output, loss of full-prefix early exits, and reputation/reset
   behavior alongside proposal commitment. Vantage's missing-authorship fallback is
   Delta-dependent. Original Starfish has responsive bounds under honest
   neighboring leaders, so test and label the precise faulty-predecessor case.

5. Byzantine leaders and recovery.
   Run crashes/silent leaders, selective proposal release, failed rounds, and
   recurring malicious leadership schedules. Measure stalls, recovery, tail
   latency, ordered-release delay, and the fate of each honest successor proposal.
   For pipelined Simple-IT, preserve the chosen RBC variant, speculation depth,
   fault bound, and parent-safety rules. Show actual bypass of a proposal before
   claiming it was permanently lost; disabled does not automatically mean lost.

6. Ablations and bottlenecks.
   Isolate cumulative reporting, proof/evidence handling, proactive metadata
   delivery, compact history, batching, and transport/CPU bottlenecks. Use valid,
   explicitly named variants. Show what each mechanism contributes and costs.
   Include long unresolved histories and retrieval/storage pressure where they
   reveal practical limitations. Removing a safety rule is not a valid speedup.

MEASUREMENT RULES

The main performance plots must measure the transaction path, not only a
preloaded ordering microbenchmark. Report submission-to-ordered-release and
submission-to-execution latency where implemented, and separately break down
batching, dissemination, evidence preparation, ordering, and payload retrieval.
If a counterpart stops at a different endpoint, expose that difference rather
than comparing unlike numbers. A payload-ready dummy execution workload must
not be described as an application execution benchmark.

Also measure proposal-to-direct-commit separately to evaluate the 3delta aligned
and 5delta unaligned bounds, and proposal/commit spacing for the ready pipeline.
Record alignment and input/history readiness; do not hide their cost by starting
the only stopwatch after preparation. Abstract message-delay bounds do not
predict real latency when serialization, crypto, queues, and bandwidth dominate.

Use open-loop offered load where appropriate; track submitted, admitted, ordered,
executed, pending, and failed requests. Avoid coordinated omission and silently
discarding slow replicas or unfinished transactions. Use unique IDs and verify
agreement, no duplicate execution, and payload integrity in benchmark runs.
Define first-correct versus all-correct endpoints and clock synchronization.

Charge serialized sends once. Internal ordering's O(kappa n^2) excludes external
descriptor/witness bytes; actual ordering traffic includes n^2|x| and n|pi|.
Availability evidence distribution costs O(kappa n^3 log T + n X_r) per
opportunity, with extra source material X_r counted. If this evidence is carried
inside the ordering proposal, do not count the same multicast a second time.
Include unsuccessful opportunities, retrieval, retransmissions, transport
overhead, and storage; amortize over distinct useful output. Track correct-sender
protocol traffic separately from adversarial traffic and actual resource use.

Match hardware, network, workload, batching, execution, durability, and fault
conditions across counterparts. Report justified differences and each baseline's
native configuration. Raptr aggregation is not necessarily threshold signing;
preserve actual crypto choices. Do not handicap a baseline to make Delugi win.
