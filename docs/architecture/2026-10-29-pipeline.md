# Delugi's one-delay proposal pipeline

The default is canonical immediate PROG, `report_schedule=pipeline`, and
immediate ordering-message bundling. The old progress-pacing and history-ready
policies remain explicit comparison settings.

## Readiness and wakeups

An opportunity `k` may open its once-only REPORT when its leader is admitted
and one of these conditions holds:

1. `k <= 3` (initial proposals with empty history).
2. The leader has completed its own HISTORY3 report through `k-3`.
3. The leader has completed its own report through `k-4` **and fully validated
   proposal `k-3`**, including immutable Q. For `k=4`, the completed genesis
   prefix substitutes for the nonexistent report at horizon zero; a validated
   proposal 1 is still required.
4. It retains `f+1` distinct, authenticated CLOSED signatures for exactly `k-3`.
5. It has the exact `n-f` HISTORY3 reports needed by proposal `k`.

The speculative branch in item 3 aligns preparation with a productive ordering
wave. A retained header, VOTE or CER alone does not establish full proposal
validation. The other branches permit recovery without requiring a proposal
from a silent leader.

`Replica::pipeline_preparation_ready` checks this predicate, and
`preparation_predecessor_validated` checks the fully verified proposal store.
Completion of own report `h` wakes opportunities `h+3` and `h+4`; full proposal
validation at `h` and the first CLOSED amplification threshold at `h` each wake
`h+3`. Existing admission and exact-history notices also retry the predicate.
Thus admission, prefix completion and proposal validation can arrive in either
order. The full-validation wake applies to direct reception and reconstruction
of a complete proposal. `Node::report` retains the once guard. The base,
observations, observation pool and completed input remain immutable; there is
no refresh or re-signing. REPORT trace markers record whether the predecessor
proposal was fully validated when the opportunity opened.

PROG admission is deliberately independent of proposal readiness. It can run
ahead during a fault. The new guard bounds future *input preparation*, not
admission or the amount of retained ordering state.

## Complete healthy send wave

Let `B_r` be the boundary at which the leader emits proposal `r`. With healthy
leaders, constant nonself message delay δ, negligible computation, available
payload/proof material, and no earlier unresolved outcomes, the following wave
is enabled. Each row keeps its own typed evidence requirement; arbitrary
messages cannot be counted toward a single generic quorum.

| Message emitted at `B_r` | Sender and recipients | Evidence required |
|---|---|---|
| PROPOSE(`r`) | Leader `L_r` → all | Admitted, immutable input passes Q, exact HISTORY3(`r-3`) for `r>3` |
| VOTE(`r-1`) | Nonleaders of `r-1` → all | Full valid proposal, admitted through `r`, once-only vote |
| CER(`r-2`) | Each replica → all | `n-f` same-digest VOTEs or `f+1` same-digest CERs, admitted through `r` |
| HISTORY3(`r-2`) | Each replica → `L_(r+1)` | Every outcome through `r-2` is terminal; attached evidence authenticates positives |
| CLOSED(`r-2`) | Each replica → all | Complete corresponding HISTORY3 is already queued |
| PROG(`r+2`) | Each replica → all | Entry to `r+2` from `n-f` PROG(`r+1`); `f+1` amplification remains immediate |
| OBS(`r+1`) | Other replicas → `L_(r+1)` | Authenticated REPORT/base; fixed signed custody observation |
| REPORT(`r+2`) | Leader `L_(r+2)` → all | Admitted; own completed prefix through `r-2` and full validation of `r-1` enable the speculative guard |

There are two local shortcuts: a leader votes for its own valid proposal at
the preceding boundary, and its own OBS is fixed when it opens REPORT. Neither
waits for an artificial self-network delay. BATCH payload streaming remains
independent of this table: ready batches are sent immediately, without receipt
quorum or per-frame ACK gating. FETCH/DATA repair is likewise event driven.
Only the designated leaders emit REPORT/PROPOSE and collect their corresponding
OBS/HISTORY3; these are not all-to-all collections of interchangeable evidence.

The table describes a logical concurrent wave, not a single cross-layer wire
frame or a global synchronization barrier. The process drains enabled effects,
bundles ready ordering messages per recipient without a fill timer, and queues
the dissemination effects. Independent transport writers pipeline frames.

## Timing argument

At `B_r`, REPORT(`r+2`) is emitted. At `B_(r+1)=B_r+δ`, remote replicas return
OBS(`r+2`). At `B_(r+2)=B_r+2δ`, its observation pool is complete. The ordering
wave also delivers HISTORY3(`r-1`) at that same boundary, so proposal `r+2`
can leave immediately. VOTE, CER and received CER quorum take the next three
delays, giving direct commitment at `B_(r+2)+3δ`. Induction gives one proposal
per δ after startup, while several proposals are in flight at once.

The added validation condition introduces no delay in this healthy induction:
at `B_r`, nonleaders receive and validate proposal `r-1`, while the preceding
vote wave enables their own CER/report through `r-2`. Both prerequisites are
therefore present at the same boundary. During startup, the leader of round 4
receives valid proposal 1 when REPORT 4 must start; treating prefix zero as
completed preserves the same schedule. It cannot start REPORT 4 unconditionally
when leader 1 is silent.

The signed-core regression checks twelve actual composed proposals and fresh
payloads, rather than deriving timestamps from a stand-alone timing model.
It checks each message type in the table, authenticated distinct signers,
matching horizons/digests and recipients, and exact once-only sends. At δ=10 ms,
its ordering start is at 20 ms after payload preloading; proposals occur at
40, 50, 60, 70, …, 150 ms, and direct commits at 70, 80, …, 180 ms.

## Bounded speculation and fault recovery

Let `C*` be the greatest contiguous own-report prefix completed by any correct
replica. Every correct REPORT opportunity satisfies `k <= C*+4`. The local
speculative path permits four positions of advance and now also requires a
validated predecessor proposal. The resolved local path and either authenticated
fallback require a correct completed prefix through `k-3`, allowing only three.
The startup rules also satisfy the bound at `C*=0`. This bounds future input
preparation independently of how far PROG admission has advanced.

For a genuinely silent round `s`, there is no valid proposal `s`, so the
speculative path cannot open REPORT(`s+3`). Its third honest anchor instead
prepares after local completion or authenticated CLOSED/history evidence for
`s`. Batches streamed during the preceding timeout can then enter its fresh
observation exchange. The input is still fixed once; the correction delays
its creation rather than changing its contents afterward.

The CLOSED fallback matters for the unchanged main timer. Under the ordering
proof's post-stabilization assumptions with δ<Δ, let `t` be the earliest main
timer start for a sufficiently late honest round `k>3`. Its CLOSED(`k-3`) quorum contains at least
`f+1` correct broadcasters. Their broadcasts reach the leader by `t+δ`, and
the leader is admitted by `t+2δ`. The fallback therefore enables a valid input
by `t+4δ`. Exact history is available by `t+3Δ+2δ`; consequently all correct
replicas send CER by `t+3Δ+4δ < t+7Δ`. The original catch-up argument and
terminal/scanner rules remain unchanged.

This fallback proof does not rely on the new speculative branch: restricting
that branch cannot remove the stated input-readiness deadline. Conversely,
full proposal validation is not a certificate that the predecessor will make
progress. A Byzantine leader can send a valid proposal selectively, trigger
speculative preparation at one later leader, and then stall. The guard removes
the absent-proposal failure demonstrated by the silent-leader experiment; it
does not establish a general bound on input age under arbitrary Byzantine
behavior or asymmetric WAN delays.

## The regression this guard corrects

The previous guard allowed REPORT(`k`) solely because the leader had completed
through `k-4`, and allowed all four startup opportunities without proposal
evidence. That bound on round distance did not bound elapsed input age. In the
[29 October WAN experiment](../../benchmarks/results/delugi-pipeline-wan-2026-10-29/README.md),
third-anchor inputs froze approximately 6.63 seconds before proposal emission.
Timeout-period arrivals missed those inputs: 2,811 of 3,000 transactions entered
the next first honest anchor and then waited for another silent round to clear
the contiguous prefix. Mean prefix wait was 6.66 seconds.

The healthy trace exhibits the same ordering mismatch on a smaller scale.
Singapore opened REPORT 65 at 9,794.871 ms after its report through 61, but only
validated proposal 62 at 9,893.288 ms. Input 65 then waited 98.105 ms for history
before its proposal. The new predecessor condition would defer that REPORT by
98.417 ms in the retained event sequence. This is a diagnosis of the old trace,
not a prediction of the complete corrected execution: changing a preparation
time also changes later observation pools, proposal contents and message timing.
The live-transaction regressions below test this correction in addition to
proposal cadence. Neither the old WAN results nor a counterfactual trace
establishes its new independent-process performance.

This is a composition scheduling correction: the two-delay input-readiness
contract starts at the bounded readiness trigger, not at arbitrary early
admission. The pinned historical TeX sources and old benchmark records are
preserved; their earlier composition trigger is not silently relabeled.

## Executable checks

`core/delugi/tests/pipeline_composition.rs` delivers real signed, serialized
messages through the production composed core. It covers the healthy typed
wave, repeated silent leaders with three honest anchors and late streamed
batches, recovery close to the timer bound, valid Byzantine forks and invalid
cross-branch grafts, and the exact authenticated CLOSED preparation guard.

`core/delugi/tests/freshness_regression.rs` adds transaction-sensitive checks:

- `batches_arriving_during_silence_use_the_same_recovery_anchor` streams exact
  batches at 250 and 1,000 ms during successive missing-leader intervals. They
  must execute in the corresponding third anchors, rounds 4 and 8, and reach
  output by those anchors' CER quorum. The old guard placed the first batch in
  round 6 and delayed output until 1,490 ms. With the corrected guard, REPORT 4
  starts at 740 ms, proposal 4 leaves at 760 ms and the batch outputs at 790 ms.
  The second batch outputs in round 8 at 1,500 ms. These are deterministic
  signed-core regression times with δ=10 ms and Δ=100 ms, not process benchmark
  measurements.
- `healthy_pipeline_keeps_delta_cadence_with_post_start_arrivals` begins with
  no preloaded payloads, then streams four origins' batches at seven-millisecond
  intervals across observation boundaries. It checks δ-spaced proposals for
  rounds 5–24, 3δ direct commitment and at most 7δ from creation to output for
  every tested transaction.
- `speculative_preparation_requires_full_exact_predecessor_validation` checks
  that a header, VOTE, single CER, invalid full proposal or valid proposal for
  another round cannot supply the speculative predecessor condition. Valid
  proposal 1 enables round 4 with the virtual genesis prefix, in both arrival
  orders relative to admission.

The existing ordering, availability, repair, scanner, transport and end-to-end
regressions remain in the workspace suite. These checks and the timing argument
establish the stated properties; they are not a machine-checked proof of all
Byzantine executions.

The [earlier local independent-process measurements](../../benchmarks/results/pipeline-2026-10-29/README.md)
record the previous guard's TCP delay, cadence and endpoint definitions. Their
latencies remain historical evidence, not measurements of this correction.
